## Why this is credible

- Event timestamps are SEC acceptance times in New York; entries are strictly after the event is known. No same-close or same-bar fill.
- Reaction measures use only bars completed before the entry open; tests mutate future bars and verify unchanged entries/reactions.
- Duplicate/companion 2.02 filings within 24 hours are collapsed before any return is computed.
- Costs are 5 bps/side base and 10 bps/side 2x on every entry and exit; PFU is year-dependent with annual netting and a 10-year loss carry.
- The strategy pays annual PFU because positions are realised within days. QQQ and BIL are buy-and-hold benchmarks, so their PFU is charged on the terminal gain only, matching the repo's `france.after_tax` convention. That is the harder (not easier) comparison for the strategy.
- Idle cash earns BIL, so the primary is not penalized for holding cash; the zero-cash-yield version is also registered.
- Every variant and failed fold is in the CSV; DSR uses 50 trials and CSCV uses the full T x 50 matrix.

## Limitations

- The universe is current large-cap filers with cached bars, not a point-in-time index. Survivorship can flatter any long-only earnings strategy.
- Daily bars cannot model the opening auction, queue position or intraday execution. Entry at the printed open is an assumption.
- After-tax annual approximation for the year table ignores loss-carry path dependence; the headline after-tax multiple uses the full path.
- Long-short ignores short-dividend payments and assumes 500 bps borrow, with a 1,000 bps sensitivity only reported if it is a candidate.

## What would change the verdict

- A point-in-time earnings-event sample with delisted/acquired names, not the current-filer cache.
- A primary that beats QQQ and BIL after tax at 2x costs with a placebo p below 0.05, DSR above 0.95 and no single year carrying the result.
- Intraday execution data showing the opening print is fillable at the size implied by 10% positions.
